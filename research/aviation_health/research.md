# Aviation and healthcare research appendix

Neither of the two fully valued companies qualifies as an attractive investment at the sampled prices. AerCap offers the stronger economic case, but its probability-weighted present value is $122.56 versus $145.17. IQVIA's value is $117.13 versus $261.17 after explicit reinvestment and stock compensation. ICON is an unconfirmed turnaround; accounting remediation could change its assessment. These are comparative research conclusions, not recommendations to short the shares.

## Discovery: follow flights through the fleet and aftermarket

The underlying paid activity is passenger and cargo transport. Airlines fund aircraft ownership, leases, maintenance and fuel out of ticket/cargo revenue and external financing. More travel creates demand for seats, flight hours and cycles; it does not create an equal percentage increase in annual aircraft purchases. Higher seats per aircraft and utilization can absorb some traffic, while aging assets create replacement and maintenance demand even without more flights.

IATA's June outlook estimates 30,407 active aircraft in May 2026 and a normal requirement of 33,577. Its effective shortage is 3,170 aircraft: higher utilization, delayed retirements and unusually high load factors compensate for it. The separately reported 5,600 hypothetical missing deliveries is a counterfactual cumulative production figure and must not be added to that shortage. Neither is an annual order flow. IATA also forecasts only a 2% industry net margin in 2026, limiting financially supported willingness to pay ever-higher rents. [IATA, June 2026, pp 27–29 and 38–39](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)

Supply is responding. Airbus delivered 351 commercial aircraft in H1; its targets include an A320 rate of 70–75 per month by end-2027 and A350 rate of 12 in 2028. Boeing delivered 314 in H1 and is transitioning the 737 rate to 47 per month; several certification-dependent deliveries remain management expectations. These are corroborating manufacturer disclosures, not proof that every planned aircraft can be delivered. [Airbus, July 29, commercial aircraft/outlook](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-airbus-reports-half-year-h1-2026-results), [Boeing, July 28, Commercial Airplanes](https://investors.boeing.com/investors/news/press-release-details/2026/Boeing-Reports-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)

The annual model starts May 31, rather than incorrectly applying a full year of additions to a May fleet. Remaining 2026 uses seven months. Normal fleet need grows by `(1 + traffic growth)/(1 + seats-per-aircraft growth)`; the latter is 1% annually. Usable fleet adds delivered, qualified aircraft and reactivations, less retirements. New purchase requirements equal the change in required fleet plus replacements less reactivations. Higher load factors are not credited again. Nameplate and usable deliveries are separate; no second utilization or qualification haircut is applied to usable deliveries.

- **Persistent scarcity:** traffic grows 5.5% in 2027–30, then 4.5%; usable deliveries rise from 1,650 in 2026 to only 2,400 in 2032. The effective deficit increases from about 2,973 at end-2026 to 4,742 in 2030 and 6,776 in 2035. Skilled engine repair, certification and component availability remain constraints after airframe factories expand.
- **Orderly normalization:** traffic grows 4% after 2026; deliveries reach 2,200 in 2027, 2,450 in 2028 and 2,650 in 2030. Retirements rise from 650 to 1,300 annually across the model. The deficit falls to 1,369 in 2028 and 104 in 2030, turning into roughly 317 spare-equivalent aircraft in 2031. This is our independent normalization path, not IATA's predicted date.
- **Oversupply or disappointment:** traffic is flat in 2027 and grows 2–3% afterward, while usable deliveries reach 2,650 in 2028. The model already has 523 spare-equivalent aircraft in 2028 despite positive traffic growth. Retirements then accelerate. Demand need not contract for lease economics to deteriorate.

These global equivalent aircraft are imperfect substitutes: widebody/narrowbody mix, geography, sanctions, airport slots, engines and financing can preserve local scarcity after the aggregate gap closes. Detailed qualified manufacturer output by aircraft/engine type is unavailable, so the dates are ranges controlled principally by traffic, usable deliveries and retirement behavior. Backlog can include cancellations and delayed financing; the model does not count all backlog as funded demand. Expansion of airframe output migrates the pressure toward engine shops, replacement parts and maintenance labor; it also eventually supplies airlines with substitutes for expensive older leases.

## AerCap: credible scarcity exposure, insufficient price gap

**Observed chain:** more flight activity and constrained deliveries encourage lease extensions and used-aircraft purchases; AerCap already has a large lease portfolio. **Inferred chain:** availability supports asset sale proceeds and selected renewals, raising income after funding, depreciation and credit losses. **Unresolved link:** fleetwide pricing benefit after contracts roll, aircraft mix changes and airlines absorb fuel costs. This uncertainty matters because most rents are fixed: a shortage does not instantly reprice the entire portfolio. AerCap actually attributed part of its 2025 basic-rent decline to extensions at lower rates. [2025 Form 20-F, February 12, 2026, p 54 and lease note](https://www.aercap.com/investors/shareholder-services/sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-007513/aer-20251231.htm)

The value-capture mechanism is scale purchasing, access to financing, fleet placement and asset trading, with long leases moderating both windfalls and downside. Aircraft leasing dominates the assets; engines, helicopters, maintenance rights and equity-method investments are modeled within the disclosed asset/revenue categories rather than assigning a small segment's premium to everything. June productive lease assets total $61.543 bn, plus $4.107 bn pre-delivery payments. AerCap owned 1,461 aircraft; H1 net-book-value-weighted utilization was 98%. Its new aircraft commitments extend into the 2030 s, so investment cannot stop at the holding horizon. [H1 report, July 29, pp 4–7 and fleet/commitments sections](https://www.aercap.com/investors/shareholder-services/sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-050456/aer-20260630_d2.htm)

The current bridge uses $42.774 bn carrying debt, $1.686 bn unrestricted cash and 154.430 m basic shares. Fair values use 157.184 m shares, including existing restricted awards. Basic equity capitalization is $22.418 bn; descriptive enterprise value is $63.505 bn. Debt is an operating funding input for this lessor, so that enterprise value is not compared with an industrial EV multiple. Maintenance/customer deposits and deferred taxes enter the required-equity/funding bridge. Restricted cash is excluded. Opening cash is already reflected in required book equity and earning power; it is not added again to the equity DCF.

Historical revenue was $7.580 bn/$7.997 bn/$8.517 bn in 2023/24/25; reported common earnings were $3.136 bn/$2.099 bn/$3.751 bn. Russian recoveries materially distort those earnings and are excluded from future operating assumptions. H1 2026 revenue was $4.409 bn and net income $1.544 bn. Management's July full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $16.80 excludes future H2 sale gains and is not our recurring EPS estimate. [2025 Form 20-F, consolidated statements](https://www.aercap.com/investors/shareholder-services/sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-007513/aer-20251231.htm), [Q2 release, July 29, guidance and reconciliation](https://www.aercap.com/investors/news-events/news/detail/643/aercap-holdings-n-v-reports-strong-financial-results-for)

The model separately forecasts basic rent as average productive assets times rental yield; maintenance and other revenue; sale gains; depreciation; impairment; funding cost; operating expenses; taxes; and equity-method earnings. Base lease assets grow 4.5% in 2027–28, tapering to 2.5%; rent yield peaks at 11.1% then normalizes to 10.6%. Funding cost rises from 4.4% to 4.8%; sale gains decline from $750 m in 2026 to $250 m in 2035. Maintenance/growth investment and prepayments follow that fleet path. Those are assumptions, not guaranteed contracts. Normalized 2035 base return on required book equity is approximately 10.6%.

For a lessor, conventional FCFF obscures operating debt funding. We use:

`FCFE = net income − increase in required common equity`

The annual cash bridge independently checks:

`FCFE = NI + D&A + impairment − all-in asset investment + disposal proceeds − sale gains + debt issuance + deposit/other funding`

All-in asset investment includes replacement assets, portfolio growth and changes in pre-delivery payments. The prepayment change also appears as `delta_nwc` for visibility and must not be deducted twice. The deposit/other-funding change is a balancing capital-structure assumption, not a gratuitous cash source: every row must equal NI less required equity growth. Both replacement and growth investment are paid. JV earnings are assumed distributable after proportionate capital retention; trapped cash remains a limitation.

Terminal value capitalizes `NI × (1+g) − ending required equity × g`, discounted at the cost of equity. Base cost is 10.5%, approximately 5.72 percentage points over the September 4 US ten-year Treasury yield of 4.78%; downside/upside costs are 12%/9.5%. Terminal growth is 1.5%/2.5%/3%, with capital growth matching in the final explicit year. No permanent peak disposal margin or book-value multiple is used. [US Treasury, September 4 row](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026)

The downside/base/upside probabilities are 30%/50%/20%, judgment weights rather than statistical frequencies. Manufacturer expansion and thin airline profitability make normalization more likely than unbroken scarcity; leveraged credit/residual risk warrants substantial downside weight. Present fair values are **$42.71/$136.03/$208.66**, weighted **$122.56**. Weighted upside is **−15.6%**. Margin of safety is defined as `1 − price/weighted value`, here **−18.4%**. Moving ten probability points from upside to downside lowers value to $105.96.

Five-year shareholder XIRRs are **−14.6%/7.7%/16.8%**. The probability-weighted average of those scenario returns is 2.8%; the IRR of expected dated cash flows is 5.3%. They are different statistics. Future equity values of $60.80/$201.80/$305.31 are September 2031 residual values, not today's intrinsic values. The return calculation pays only remaining assumed dividends (the September 3, 2026 dividend predates purchase), retains residual FCFE at zero interest and adds it once to the remaining business value. It assumes no buyback accretion or additional future award dilution: future SBC is retained as an economic operating expense. Terminal value contributes 40%/47%/53% of present equity value.

**Reverse valuation:** “The current price can be justified if average portfolio rental yields are about 21 basis points higher than our base path, holding capital, sale gains, funding and credit costs fixed.” The corresponding normalized yield is 10.81%, versus 10.60%. “My forecast differs because manufacturer output should eventually relieve the fleet deficit and long contracts slow repricing.” “The evidence supporting that difference is the disclosed production ramps and AerCap's own lower-rate extensions.” “The resulting difference in value is $9.13 per share versus the base case and $22.61 versus weighted value.” This small reverse-yield disagreement is within ordinary forecast uncertainty; AerCap is not demonstrably cheap. At 10.5% equity cost, a ±50 bp yield change produces $113.82–$158.24. Holding base operations but using 9.5% or 11.5% equity cost produces $154.53 or $121.61.

The strongest opposing case to a positive AerCap thesis combines faster delivery ramps, weak airline budgets, higher refinancing costs and falling used-aircraft values. The downside raises 2027 impairment to $900 m, eventually funding cost to 5.4%, reduces rental yield and introduces disposal losses. It values shares at $42.71. This is not merely a one-variable stress. The pandemic is a relevant counterexample: AerCap's 2020 common loss was $298.6 m with $1.087 bn of asset impairment. GECAS later changed its scale, and the next downturn need not resemble a shutdown, but the episode demonstrates that scarce-looking long-lived capacity can become surplus while debt remains. [2021 Form 20-F, March 30, 2022, pp 53–55 and F-7](https://www.aercap.com/investors/shareholder-services/sec-filings/content/0001378789-22-000006/aer-20211231.htm)

Observable tests: by FY2026/Q2 2027, monitor portfolio rent yield, utilization, impairment and funding spread. Base requires roughly 97% or better utilization and near-11% rent yield in 2027; two quarters below 95% would materially weaken it. Global usable deliveries near 2,650 in 2028 with traffic below 3% would support the oversupply path. Impairment near the $900 m stress combined with funding above 5% and rent yield near 10% would invalidate the base and require the full adverse valuation. Delivery delays alone do not validate the thesis if airline payment quality deteriorates.

## Healthcare: trial demand can recover without service scarcity

The economic activity is funded drug development and subsequent regulated testing. Pharmaceutical R&D budgets and biotech financing pay for trials, laboratory work, data and manufacturing support to establish safety/effectiveness and reach commercial sales. More approved drugs is not directly equal to more annual trials; failures, study design, enrollment, outsourcing and duration mediate spending. Large-pharma budgets and biotech financing also cannot simply be added without considering licensing transfers and double counting.

IQVIA's primary authored 2025 report records 5,302 trial starts in 2023 and 5,318 in 2024, well below the 6,432 peak in 2021. Its March 2026 public summary does not describe a uniform 2025 recovery: funding declined and oncology starts fell 4%, although later-phase oncology activity improved. The public summary does not expose a verified aggregate 2025 total. [IQVIA, March 2025, printed pp 14–15, mirrored report](https://makingpharmaindustry.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/iqvia-institute-rd-trends-2025-forweb.pdf), [IQVIA, March 25, 2026, public findings](https://www.iqvia.com/insights/the-iqvia-institute/reports-and-publications/reports/global-r-and-d-trends-2026)

The clinical scenario CSV assumes 5,200 starts in 2025 solely as a transparent calibration. Annual active service-years combine cohorts with weights 0.5/1.0/0.75/0.25 for the current and three prior starting years. Outsourced work equals active service-years times outsourcing share times complexity divided by efficiency. Trial-start growth is 0.5%/3%/4.5%; base complexity rises 2%, efficiency 1%, outsourcing gains 0.5 percentage point annually and price rises 2%. Thus efficiency lowers input intensity while total work can still rise. In the adverse case efficiency improves 2.5% against only 1% complexity growth; no automatic demand rebound is assumed.

Global qualified CRO capacity is not publicly measured. The model therefore uses an explicitly assumed 8,000 standardized service-year capacity anchor with 2.5–3.5% annual expansion, not a claimed observed bottleneck. At a $5 m initial fee per standardized service-year, it is a budget envelope, not a census of trial revenue. Base utilization rises from about 93% in 2026 to 98% in 2030 and 106% in 2035; above 100% signals a need for staffing or deferred work, not immediately realizable sales. Adverse utilization falls to 67%. There is insufficient evidence to claim aggregate CRO scarcity or precise industry market share from these modeled units.

Customer economics and substitution matter. Standardized data processing can lower billable work, sponsors can redesign protocols or bring work in-house, and regulators may accept different evidence packages. FDA's June revised draft discusses a single adequate study plus confirmatory evidence in appropriate circumstances; it is draft guidance, not an already implemented uniform reduction in trial requirements. [FDA, June 2026, summary and implementation status](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/demonstrating-substantial-evidence-effectiveness-human-drug-and-biological-products)

## IQVIA: operating recovery does not support the sampled valuation

**Observed:** IQVIA sells clinical outsourcing and commercial analytics; Q2 net new R&D bookings were $3.15 bn and book-to-bill 1.22. **Inferred:** funded starts, complexity and outsourcing support mid-single-digit underlying work growth, with proprietary data and integration supporting renewal economics. **Unresolved:** durable pricing and staffing productivity after competition and automation; absolute global qualified capacity and IQVIA's physical share are not disclosed. Customers may capture productivity gains through lower fees. Backlog of $34.2 bn is a multi-year stock, of which management expects $9.2 bn to convert in the next 12 months; it is not all next year's revenue. [Q2 results, July 28, bookings and segment sections](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)

The model respects the January 2026 reorganization into Commercial Solutions and R&D Solutions. Base 2026 revenue is $7.10 bn/$10.25 bn, totaling $17.35 bn, close to management's $17.275–$17.475 bn guidance. Commercial is approximately 41% of modeled revenue, so attractive data economics are not assigned to all clinical services. Forecast standardized workload equivalents are revenue-calibrated quantities, explicitly not observed employees or actual trials. R&D volume and price growth are separated, but pass-through reimbursement and project mix make these units imperfect.

Revenue grew from $14.984 bn in 2023 to $15.405 bn in 2024 and $16.310 bn in 2025; GAAP EBIT was $1.977 bn/$2.202 bn/$2.182 bn. H1 2026 revenue was $8.519 bn and EBIT $1.020 bn. This is a recovery in sales with mixed profit conversion. Operating cash flow and cash capex were $2.654 bn and $603 m in 2025; SBC was $247 m. The model retains SBC in GAAP operating costs instead of treating its cash-flow addback as owner cash. [2025 Form 10-K, February 17, consolidated statements and software notes](https://s201.q4cdn.com/580005511/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/IQV-2025-12-31-10K_Filed-with-exhibits.pdf), [Q2 2026 results, financial tables](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)

The equity bridge uses 164.6 m basic shares as of July 21 plus 1.2 m incremental awards, $15.999 bn debt, $1.909 bn cash less $600 m operating liquidity, $389 m pension/minority claims and $482.25 m discounted nonoperating investments. The latter equals 75% of disclosed securities and joint-venture carrying values; no nonoperating affiliate earnings are also capitalized. The share date and debt bridge are corroborated by the [July 28 Form 10-Q, cover and condensed balance sheet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1478242/000162828026050211/iqv-20260630.htm). Operating leases remain in operating expenses. Gross underfunded pensions are included without assuming that pension surpluses can be freely transferred. Basic market capitalization is $42.989 bn; operating-adjusted current EV is $57.585 bn, or $56.985 bn using all cash rather than the operating reserve.

Base revenue grows about 6% early, tapering to 3%; Commercial EBIT margin reaches 21%, R&D 14.5%, less corporate costs. Taxes are 23%. Reported-style capex starts at 4% of revenue and working capital consumes 8% of incremental sales. The common engine adds explicit 2036–40 years: nominal sales and costs grow at terminal g while all-in reinvestment phases toward the greater of existing net investment and `NOPAT × g/ROIC`. This recognizes replacement of amortized software, purchased data and workflow assets without releasing existing replacement investment. The additional capital is an analyst economic assumption, not disclosed capex guidance. Its annual rows are exported. The terminal 15% ROIC is uncertain; the transition prevents both a perpetual amortization windfall and a sudden forecast-to-terminal cash-flow cliff.

DCF uses FCFF = EBIT after operating tax + D&A − all-in capex − change in operating working capital. Base WACC is 9.5%, terminal growth 2.5%; adverse/upside WACCs are 11%/9% and growth 1%/3%. Dollar discount rates reflect the Treasury anchor plus business risk and debt funding; they are judgment estimates, not observed required returns. Probabilities are 30%/50%/20% because improving bookings support recovery, while flat operating profit and mixed trial data argue against overweighting the upside.

Present values are **$23.66/$131.09/$222.47**, weighted **$117.13**, about **55.2% below** the sampled price. Five-year scenario shareholder XIRRs are **−23.3%/−3.1%/6.3%**; IRR of expected cash flows is **−4.9%**. IQVIA pays no modeled dividend; residual FCFE is retained once and added to remaining operating EV, after debt and claims. Static diluted shares with SBC expensed approximate cash compensation or repurchases sufficient to offset future awards. Actual repurchase accretion is not assumed. Terminal value after 2040 contributes 24%/36%/43% of present EV. Moving ten probability points from upside to downside lowers weighted value to $97.25. Base sensitivity across 8.5–10.5% WACC and 1.5–3.5% growth is $99.36–$183.23. The margin of safety is −123.0%, using the same price-to-value definition as AER.

**Reverse valuation:** “The current price can be justified if EBIT margins are approximately 10.15 percentage points above our base throughout, with base revenue growth, explicit investment and discount assumptions otherwise unchanged.” The corresponding 2030 group margin is 25.93%, compared with our 15.77%. “My forecast differs because staffing, data maintenance, competition and sponsor efficiency savings limit profit conversion.” “The evidence supporting that difference is the recent sales/GAAP EBIT divergence and uneven trial-start recovery, corroborated by ICON's Q2 EBITDA decline.” “The resulting difference in value is $130.08 per share versus our base and $144.04 versus weighted value.” This is one price-justifying combination, not a uniquely identified market forecast or a claim about unavailable consensus.

The strongest bull case is faster funded trial starts, greater outsourcing, more complex studies and proprietary analytics delivering both growth and a sustained margin increase. That coherent upside still values shares below the sampled price. The opposing case combines trial postponements, two-sided pricing pressure, efficiency reducing service intensity, clinical labor underutilization and additional working-capital demand; it produces the $23.66 downside. Historical trial starts declined sharply after 2021 while service capacity was built, showing why a promising demand trend can still disappoint suppliers. By FY2027, two quarters of R&D book-to-bill below one, underlying revenue growth below 2% and group margin below 12% would invalidate the base recovery. Conversely, durable mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth accompanied by margins above 18% and cash conversion after SBC would require an upward revision. Bookings alone are insufficient.

## Comparative rejections and remaining gaps

The screen deliberately includes indirect beneficiaries. AAR distributes parts and supplies repair/software; HEICO provides qualified lower-cost substitute parts; MTU expands engine maintenance; Lufthansa owns a valuable repair business within an airline group. In healthcare, Charles River participates earlier in research and later in manufacturing testing, Danaher and Thermo Fisher supply recurring consumables and infrastructure, and Eurofins and Sonic operate laboratory networks. Every row in `screened.json` supplies a dated primary role source, mechanism and a specific advance/reject reason. Screen multiples are deliberately labeled rough comparisons, often annualized quarterly adjusted EPS, and do not substitute for intrinsic valuations.

The strongest unresolved alternative is ICON: July bookings were encouraging but Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 21.7%. Its investigation was concluded in May; it identified historical improper revenue adjustments and material control weaknesses. It is the remediation and historical comparability that remain unconfirmed, not the investigation's existence. A successful operational and control recovery could make the rejected comparison materially stronger. [ICON, July 29, Q2 results](https://investor.iconplc.com/node/18466/pdf), [ICON, May 27, investigation conclusion](https://investor.iconplc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/icon-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-and)

Evidence limitations are material but explicit: some non-US listings lack a verified native-share/ADR/FX bridge; the Eurofins detailed release was inaccessible; global qualified CRO supply and customer-level budgets are not observed; AerCap's aircraft mix cannot be perfectly represented by global equivalent units; IQVIA's long-run economic refresh spending is not separately disclosed; June balance sheets predate September prices; and aligned consensus is unavailable. None of these missing inputs is filled by an invented fact. AER's stronger causal chain is outweighed by limited valuation headroom; IQV's sampled price needs much stronger economics than the modeled recovery. Shared adverse dependencies are higher financing costs and tighter customer budgets, although airline and pharmaceutical spending respond to different underlying activities.
