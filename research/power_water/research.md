The starting economic change is greater electricity load, geographically different load centers and replacement of aging networks. Utilities recover capital through regulated rates; industrial users pay from project budgets to obtain reliable power. Water utilities similarly spend to maintain pressure, reduce leakage and avoid failures. Water gallons sold can be flat while repair equipment purchases rise: the installed network ages independently of water consumption. The affordability constraint matters. American Water's July 29 disclosure supports actual investment, but its $3.7 billion plan includes acquisitions; treating all of that as supplier orders would overstate demand. Census July starts/completions show a soft new-housing channel. EPA's $625 billion twenty-year need is a condition assessment, not financed annual orders. [American Water, investment and regulated businesses](https://ir.amwater.com/news-and-events/financial-releases/financial-release-details/2026/AMERICAN-WATER-REPORTS-SOLID-SECOND-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS-AFFIRMS-2026-EPS-GUIDANCE-AND-LONG-TERM-TARGETS/default.aspx), [Census, 18 August 2026](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/), [EPA, seventh assessment](https://www.epa.gov/dwsrf/epas-7th-drinking-water-infrastructure-needs-survey-and-assessment).

The grid constraint is real, but durable producer excess returns are less certain. DOE's August 10 report identifies customization, imported components and domestic manufacturing limits. Its proposed standardization, refurbishment and power electronics also describe ways the constraint can weaken. Eaton's September 2 announcement explicitly doubles Fibrebond US enclosure capacity through a $242 million-plus Arkansas investment. Enclosure capacity is not interchangeable with every Powell component, but it is competitive supply in integrated project delivery. No operating or qualification date was disclosed; the supply model therefore varies the ramp rather than treating announced output as operating capacity. [DOE, 10 August 2026](https://www.energy.gov/oe/articles/strengthening-americas-grid-supply-chain), [Eaton, 2 September 2026](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-expands-manufacturing-for-modular-electrical-enclosures.html).

The annual physical stress tests are reproducible in demand_supply.py. Electricity starts from 4,200 TWh annual US end-use consumption, explicitly distinct from 4,430 TWh generation. Activity growth is reduced by efficiency; demand is converted to peak connection duty through an assumed 60% load factor, with separate replacement and redundancy requirements. Annual supply paths are in the same standardized connection-duty unit, already adjusted for qualification/ramp. They are conditional assumptions because panel counts, supplier capacity and product mix are not publicly reconciled. They cannot establish a confirmed shortage expiry date. The slower supply path sustains tightness through 2028, normalizes around 2029, and may shift the constraint to deployment labor, grid permission and fuel access. The orderly path eases earlier; the disappointment path has excess supply despite growing electricity use. These dates are conditional results, not factual forecasts.

Water demand is modeled as existing miles times renewal rate plus new mains from housing activity. Renewal does not increase the installed stock. The 2.2 million-mile anchor is older secondary ASCE evidence reported by EPA, counted once; the 0.5% renewal-rate anchor is historic EPA survey practice and is not asserted as a current national observation. Base desired work grows from roughly 16,200 miles in 2026 to 21,309 in 2035. Financed, usable work is the minimum of desired demand, assumed installation capacity and a budget/cost constraint. Better pressure management and rehabilitation can extend pipe life, reducing replacement intensity; budget savings need not all be spent on more units. Illustrative six valves per mile and $3,000 per valve show component economics but do not establish Mueller's entire addressable market. Usable valve-foundry capacities and current installation-crew supply remain unresolved. [EPA, legacy renewal methodology, p.24](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-10/documents/corrected_sixth_drinking_water_infrastructure_needs_survey_and_assessment.pdf).

Powell comparison: backlog is an observed claim on future work; the profitability and repeatability of replacement orders are assumptions. The chain is: new power-intensive projects [observed] → custom electrical-system orders [observed] → supply tightness and qualified engineering capacity [inferred, competitor capacity incomplete] → 2026–28 revenue conversion [management backlog disclosure plus analyst timing] → sustained margins after expansion [unresolved] → FCFF after capex/working capital [calculated] → current shareholder value [calculated].

The detailed model splits revenue into oil/gas, electric utilities, commercial/industrial, petrochemical, and rail/other. No costs by end market are published; applying one consolidated EBIT margin across these categories is an allocation convention, not evidence that segment economics are identical. Commercial/industrial sales grow from $250 million in FY2026 to $850 million in FY2030 in the base case—already an optimistic operating development—while margin normalization prevents today's best economics from being capitalized indefinitely. Its upside case reaches much higher sales and still only roughly justifies today's price. Engineering and qualification support value capture; large customer projects, fixed-price execution, scarce labor, component procurement and competing integrated vendors weaken the margin claim.

The 2023–25 record shows operating margin moved from 8.9% to 17.7% to 19.7%; the lower 2023 base is evidence against assuming a structural 20%-plus floor. Maintenance versus growth capex is not separately supported. Both are deducted; the model increases spending well above the recent $13 million annual rate to support the assumed expansion. SBC remains an expense. Existing share dilution is included; future SBC is treated as cash-equivalent economic compensation rather than added back and diluted again. [Powell FY2025 10-K, pp.39–43 and segment note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80420/000008042025000152/powl-20250930.htm).

“The current price can be justified if” the strong upside revenue path is delivered, or base volumes support about 15.3 percentage points more EBIT margin throughout the model. That fixed-revenue reverse example needs 34.8% consolidated EBIT margin in FY2030. “My forecast differs because” new supply and project mix eventually constrain returns on a finite engineering and manufacturing capability. “The evidence supporting that difference is” Eaton's capacity expansion, Powell's much lower prior margins and concentration of exceptional orders. “The resulting difference in value is” approximately $83.0 per share against the base case. This reverse solution is one mathematical combination, not a uniquely inferred market expectation.

The strongest opposing view to rejecting Powell is that modular on-site generation orders are a new repeatable product category: engineering content scales more than electricity consumption, large customers prefer proven integrators, and funded customer advances enable much higher capital turnover than assumed. Our cash reserve could be overly cautious. Giving back its entire $400.36 million reserve would add only $10.94 per modeled share; that alone does not close the valuation gap. To invalidate the rejection, evidence would need to support repeated large awards, an expanded qualified workforce and much higher sustainable sales/returns. A scenario with base revenue reduced 15%, margins reduced three percentage points and capex raised 20% from 2028 values the shares at $68.17.

Mueller comparison: economic network deterioration [observed] → utility renewal budgets [partly observed] → valve/hydrant shipments [observed, presently not growing strongly] → specified-product pricing and foundry productivity [observed recently, persistence inferred] → margin after capex and corporate expense [assumed] → FCFF and value [calculated]. “The current price can be justified if” base sales/capital needs deliver about 1.9 percentage points more EBIT margin across forecast years, giving 22.9% consolidated margin in 2030. “My forecast differs because” recent pricing/productivity outperformance coexists with flat physical demand, construction softness and higher reinvestment. “The evidence supporting that difference is” the Q3 price/volume split, customer funding constraints and the multi-year financial history. “The resulting difference in value is” $2.16 per share against the base case. This is a modest expectations gap, insufficient for a positive recommendation.

The strongest case against rejecting Mueller is persistent favorable pricing, foundry execution and a funded renewal cycle raising volumes from a soft base. It has the balance sheet to tolerate delays. The strongest bear case combines distributor bargaining, soft housing and failed foundry productivity. The same 15% revenue/three-point margin/20% capex stress from 2028 reduces base fair value to $13.48. Monitor fiscal 2027 shipment volumes and cash conversion; a sustained total EBIT margin above 23% with positive volume and capex staying below 5% of sales would challenge the conservative view. Two years of flat/down volumes, worsening inventory turns or operating cash consistently below earnings despite unchanged customer terms invalidate an acceleration thesis.

Probabilities are judgmental rather than statistically estimated: 50% orderly normalization, 30% combined adverse conditions and 20% persistent exceptional economics. The downside is a joint path, not one-variable sensitivity. Both companies share utility/industrial capital spending, construction labor, materials and financing dependencies. Neither provides diversification against broad infrastructure budget disappointment.

Historical counterexample: the US gas generation construction wave around 2000–05 left extensive usable capacity whose utilization depended on fuel economics and later demand. EIA reports a 43% combined-cycle capacity factor in 2011. Today's qualified equipment and contractual backlogs differ from merchant generation, but delayed supply investment can arrive after customers moderate orders. Badger Meter's current deployment delays are a closer water example: secular AMI need coexists with lower near-term revenue. Neither history proves an impending crash; both reject perpetual-shortage assumptions. [EIA historical capacity](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=34172), [Badger July 22 results](https://investors.badgermeter.com/news-releases/press-release-details/2026/Badger-Meter-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx).
