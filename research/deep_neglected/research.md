**Research conclusion, September 8, 2026.** Grandstand offers a potentially large cash-value gap, but remains an **unconfirmed research candidate** because loss of rights to its acquired data business, unresolved financial controls, or failed refinancing can erase common equity. Opera is a better financed business at a modest discount; the evidence does not support calling it extremely undervalued at the current price. Twelve distinct issuers received a current, primary-source screen. The full comparison is in [screened.json](screened.json), including healthcare, remittances, industrial components and advertising. GAMB and GRSD are one issuer, not two screen entries.

**Opera: what the business actually earns.** The company's August 19 release reports H1 revenue $353.839m, operating profit $55.672m, CFO $64.337m and $9.291m cash equipment/development spending. Its reported cash flow adds back $12.949m equity awards, and its $6.3m OPay valuation gain produces no operating cash. June cash was $145.214m, before the $35.6m July dividend; the bridge deducts that known payment. Q2 advertising represented 65% of revenue and query 35%; monthly active users were 288m. These operating categories have different economics. Their modeled margins are allocations, because separate audited segment profits are not disclosed. [Opera Q2 2026, August 19, highlights and statements](https://investor.opera.com/news-releases/news-release-details/opera-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-beyond-high-end).

Three-year history is in the inputs: 2023/2024/2025 revenue $396.827m/$480.648m/$614.825m; operating profit $62.890m/$92.262m/$90.072m. The 2025 margin declined despite rapid growth. Equity compensation rose and third-party advertising inventory became a larger part of revenue. The model retains recurring SBC, sales spending and paid inventory, and does not apply query economics to the whole advertising business. 2024 equipment investment included a $19.1m data cluster; capital renewal is retained after that unusually large purchase. The 20-F reports one ADS per ordinary share, search agreements ending December 31, 2026 outside the US and January 31, 2027 in the US, and effective remediated financial controls at December 2025. [Opera 2025 Form 20-F, March 27, 2026, pp.25–26,36–46,61–62; notes 1,3,4,9–13](https://opera.gcs-web.com/static-files/75f6e636-cfb9-4d76-a034-9219a8f1c930).

The causal chain is: **observed** installed browser use and repeat advertiser payments → **inferred** more high-value engagement and measurable conversions → **inferred** advertiser/query budgets remain economically worthwhile → **inferred** revenue grows faster than employees and fixed product development → **calculated** higher cash after inventory, sales, compensation, taxes and development → **observed/inferred** dividends and retained cash benefit current shareholders. Query contract renewals, partner rates, future user mix and the economics of new AI interactions are **unresolved**. There is no physical bottleneck premium in this thesis.

Base demand uses 288m average monthly users in 2026, rising about 2–2.5% annually early on. Query revenue is users multiplied by full-year revenue per average user: $246m initially, growing 12%, 10%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, 4%, 3%, 3% thereafter. This combined monetization measure includes query frequency, geography, architecture, partner share and price; it is not an invented price per search. Advertising reaches users outside Opera's own browsers. Its model starts with an assumed 520m average monthly reachable users, informed by the issuer's disclosure that December 2025 reach exceeded half a billion, and multiplies reach by annual advertising revenue per reachable user. Advertising growth slows from 18% in 2027 to 5% in 2035. Reach is not an independently measured inventory census. Neither MAUs nor annualized quarterly ARPU is treated as annual purchases.

Advertisers pay from acquisition/marketing budgets for attributable conversions. Better targeting may reduce impressions needed per sale, while higher conversion makes additional campaigns affordable. The forecast therefore permits monetization growth without assuming unlimited impression growth. It does not assume AI efficiency automatically creates sufficient replacement queries. Distribution partners and dominant search providers can capture the efficiency gains. Advertising inventory and cloud capacity can respond quickly; more supply tends to lower prices, while traffic acquisition and ad-platform partners can absorb revenue growth. Base margins retain those costs. No paid relationship with a named new AI provider is assumed from a product integration announcement.

Base 2026/2030/2035 revenue is $738m/$1,209m/$1,644m, EBIT $120m/$208m/$283m and FCFF $82m/$150m/$210m. Advertising EBIT margin rises from 7.5% to 10.5%, query from 33.7% to 36%; the consolidated margin settles near 17.2%. The full-year revenue anchor is management's guidance midpoint, whereas future growth and margins are independent assumptions. DA excluding lease amortization is 2.4% of revenue; equipment/development capex is 3.1%; incremental working capital consumes 7% of positive revenue increases. Cash rent is included in EBIT, so lease liabilities are excluded from debt. Normal operating tax is 20%, not the tax rate distorted by investment gains. Forecast capex includes development and an allowance for noncash additions; separate capitalized-award amounts are not disclosed. The model does not deduct SBC twice.

The current basic equity capitalization is $1,639m. Operating enterprise value after the cash reserve and discounted nonoperating assets is $1,344m; conventional EV with all modeled cash deducted is $35m lower. The base bridge adds $74.614m excess cash, $225.675m OPay value and subtracts $6m commitments/payables, with 91.076m diluted shares. OPay is valued at 75% of its $300.9m carrying value as an explicit uncertainty haircut, not an independent valuation of the unlisted company. All other minority investments receive zero value. Kunlun's control, related-party capital allocation and uncertain OPay realization prevent treating the entire balance as a readily distributable cash substitute.

Downside/base/upside fair values today are **$4.13/$23.94/$36.67**, with 30%/50%/20% subjective weights. Weighted value **$20.54**, upside **11.5%**, margin of safety **10.3%**, defined as `1 − price/weighted present value`. Base upside is 29.9%, not 50%. WACC is 12%/10.5%/10%, terminal growth 0.5%/2.5%/3%, and terminal ROIC 18%/25%/28%. The base USD discount rate is 5.72 percentage points above the September 4 ten-year Treasury yield of 4.78%, reflecting platform, governance and customer risks. No rate assumes a return to zero rates. Terminal value supplies 37.5% of base EV, after explicit normalization. [Treasury daily curve, September 4, 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026).

“The current price can be justified if” the base volume/mix path occurs but every segment earns 4.32 percentage points less EBIT margin, implying roughly 12.9% consolidated margin in 2030. “My forecast differs because” product monetization grows faster than shared overhead while paid-inventory costs remain substantial. “The evidence supporting that difference is” the latest growth in operating profit and the existing recurring user base, tempered by the adverse 2025 margin history. “The resulting difference in value is” about $5.51 per share in the base case and $2.11 probability weighted. This is one reverse-DCF combination, not a uniquely revealed market forecast. Direct comparable consensus estimates were unavailable.

The opposing case is strong: a 25% permanent reduction in query payments from 2027, with 60% cash contribution lost, cuts base value to **$18.79**; a 50% cut yields **$13.45**. Three percentage points of higher ad inventory/partner cost lowers value to **$21.17**. Zero OPay value alone leaves **$21.46**. The combined downside assumes lower users, collapsing query payments, weaker advertising, impaired margins, continuing reinvestment and no OPay value; it is much more severe than a one-variable sensitivity. The probability-weighted value falls to **$17.29** if ten probability points move from upside to downside.

Dated-return calculations assume dividends in January and July from 2027, with payments limited to available excess cash; these dates and future dividends are assumptions. The actual July 2026 dividend is deducted once from opening cash and is not paid again to a buyer today. Buybacks are not an extra return stream: remaining cash is retained and shares are held constant after existing dilution. The model pays the outstanding repurchase/fund commitments once. Base five-year IRR is about 15.4%; applying a 20%/40% discount to future operating equity lowers it to about 11.7%/7.5%. Probability-weighted cash-flow IRRs are about 11.6%/8.0%/3.8%. These are not present fair values or guaranteed realizations. The arithmetic average of scenario IRRs is much lower because bad outcomes matter.

Observable tests: by Q1 2027, verify Google renewals and realized query revenue per user; a sustained 25% payment reduction eliminates almost the whole base gap. Through 2027, monitor users, ad cost of inventory, consolidated IFRS margin and actual cash after development/SBC. Two quarters of shrinking query monetization with ad margin below 5% would move the case toward downside. An independent OPay cash exit helps but is unnecessary for basic operating value; the model assigns no specific exit date. At current price, Opera remains a useful comparison rather than the strongest extreme-value recommendation.

**Grandstand: a potentially large gap with fragile financing.** The issuer's name changed July 16, 2026; its new symbol is GRSD. H1 revenue was $78.204m, operating profit $2.095m, CFO $2.702m. H1 CFO includes $9.784m old acquisition consideration and $2.813m acquired-employee bonuses. Removing the purchase payment is legitimate only if the outstanding acquisition liability is deducted elsewhere. The model does so and still deducts recurring SBC, development, cash rent and future bonus settlement. The $27.966m contractual acquisition payment due April 2027 is larger than its discounted $26.479m balance. June principal debt was $126.438m, cash $8.809m, available revolver $24.5m; bank debt matures February 28, 2028. June covenants were compliant. Basic shares were 35.597m; the model adds all 2.603m outstanding RSUs. [Grandstand H1 Form 6-K, August 13, 2026, pp.2–6,12,20–28](https://grandstand.com/investors/media/document/e380013d-af9f-4fb0-aee0-e9204372f868/assets/d293d430-1bb9-4014-98c1-3cba4702ff92.pdf?disposition=inline).

Marketing revenue is eroding, but the operational evidence is more nuanced than falling headline sales. Management says non-SEO sources now produce 67% of marketing revenue and its completed 25% headcount reduction saves $13m annually. That is a management forecast, not proven savings. New depositing referrals increased while blended revenue per referral fell, so a higher referral count alone does not mean better economics. Base marketing revenue declines another 8% in 2027, 4% in 2028 and 2% in 2029, despite management's more optimistic expectation of growth in 2027. [Grandstand Q2 management call, August 13, 2026, pp.2–5](https://grandstand.com/investors/media/document/f609d165-085f-481b-9e8e-39ab434073cc/assets/GRSD_Q2_2026_Transcript.pdf?disposition=inline).

The valuable alternative activity is supplying structured odds, events, order books and sports information to operators, market makers and consumers. API documentation confirms products for streaming and mapping prediction-market information. It does not establish permission to redistribute every source feed, how much customers pay, or a contractual customer relationship with any exchange mentioned in technical examples. [OpticOdds technical documentation, accessed September 8, 2026](https://developer.opticodds.com/docs/opticodds-for-prediction-market-makers). Independent competitor Sportradar reports growing betting-data revenue and new prediction-market relationships, corroborating actual spending and also formidable competing supply; some of its growth is acquired and cannot be treated as organic industry growth. [Sportradar Q2 results, August 3, 2026, revenue and business highlights](https://investors.sportradar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sportradar-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results).

The causal chain is: **observed** more digital betting/trading products and live-data integrations → **inferred** operator technology and risk-management budgets fund reliable information → **observed** Grandstand data subscriptions increase → **inferred** recurring delivery scales faster than cloud, engineering and service costs → **calculated** owner cash increases after SBC, software investment and debt interest → **unresolved** creditors, acquired-business sellers and data-rights claimants leave enough for current common shareholders. There is no assertion of scarce server capacity. Access rights, software reliability and distribution are the constraints; their ownership and cost are more decisive than physical output.

Marketing demand uses estimated annual NDCs of 0.520m in 2026 against observed H1 0.265m, then modest growth even while value per referral and retained-cohort income fall. Marketing revenue per NDC includes revenue share from earlier cohorts, advertising and ticketing, so it is a diagnostic rather than a claimed CPA price. Sports-data revenue begins at $46.5m and grows 18%, 16%, 14%, 12%, 10%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, reaching $114m by 2035. Actual paying seats, enterprise contract count, usage units and retention rates are undisclosed. The annual data revenue index is provided, but this is an evidence gap rather than a fabricated customer census. Increasing customers, higher event coverage and greater intensity can produce the base growth; they are not independently identified. Competitive supply can scale quickly unless rights restrict it, and larger rights owners may capture the gains. No revenue or option value is assigned to the new Rollcard product.

Three-year reported revenue was $108.652m/$127.182m/$165.447m and CFO $17.910m/$37.638m/$19.104m for 2023/2024/2025. Data revenue jumped after OddsJam was acquired; historical acquisition growth is not extrapolated as organic. Reported 2025 operating loss includes acquisition consideration remeasurement and domain impairment. [2025 Form 20-F, March 19, 2026, Items 3,5,15 and financial statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839799/000183979926000048/gamb-20251231.htm).

Base 2026/2030/2035 revenue is $167.5m/$186.0m/$229.5m; EBIT $22.5m/$40.9m/$56.9m; FCFF $24.1m/$32.6m/$38.8m. Nonlease DA includes a separately declining $9.5m acquired-amortization stream, reaching zero over seven years, plus recurring depreciation/amortization at 2.1% of revenue. Ongoing capex is 4% of revenue, materially above current recurring depreciation. It includes software, equipment and noncash capital additions. Cash contribution margins after SBC and rent reach 25% marketing and 32% data before DA and explicit corporate/legal costs. Taxes rise from 20% to 23% as allowances normalize. **The strongest opposing case changes the conclusion.** Swish seeks injunctive relief and damages against OddsJam/OpticOdds over alleged misuse of proprietary odds information. The company disputes the allegations and cannot estimate the outcome. The latest primary report described an August 27 hearing on leave to bring counterclaims; a primary subsequent ruling was not established. Assertions in repeated commentary about rights, named customers or damages were not used as facts. [Grandstand H1 Form 6-K, August 13, 2026, note 25, p.39](https://grandstand.com/investors/media/document/e380013d-af9f-4fb0-aee0-e9204372f868/assets/d293d430-1bb9-4014-98c1-3cba4702ff92.pdf?disposition=inline).

The subsidiary's August 5 update presents the strongest issuer opposition: it alleges antitrust violations by Swish and challenges the basis for the original claim. These are allegations, not judicial findings. A secondary transcription seen in the parent research reports leave to bring counterclaims was granted August 27; that procedural step does not decide data ownership or the requested injunction. No primary merits resolution was established. [OpticOdds litigation update, August 5, 2026](https://opticodds.com/blog/opticodds-litigation-update).

An explicit injunction stress cuts data sales 70% from 2027, reduces its margin 15 percentage points, and adds $3m annual legal/repair costs. Operating EV falls to **$130.8m** and common equity to **zero**, before any damages. A separate $25m/$50m/$100m additional claim with base operations intact gives **$3.51/$2.86/$1.55** per share; those amounts are stresses, not estimates of damages. Marketing suffering an additional 10% annual erosion and five margin points of pressure gives **$1.64**. The combined downside has both shrinking marketing and data, costly remediation and unavailable refinancing, also producing zero common value.

The cash settlement stress is especially important. Paying the $27.966m deferred consideration in April 2027 and scheduled bank amortization needs about **$23.5m** of the June **$24.5m** unused revolver in the base case, before any additional litigation outflow. It then requires refinancing about **$125.6m** in February 2028. A 2% financed fee and 9% interest on the replacement loan are explicit assumptions. The combined downside runs out of disclosed availability by April 2027. Alternatively, settling the acquisition in shares at today's price adds roughly **14.6m** shares and avoids the cash payment; that burden is counted through dilution instead of subtracting the same claim twice. The option dilution sensitivity assuming exercise of every existing option, including all unearned founder tranches, reduces upside value to about **$10.83**.

Base/upside five-year IRRs with explicit cash settlement and refinancing are approximately **33%/58%**; downside is **−100%**. A 20%/40% exit discount to future operating equity produces base IRRs about **29%/24%**, and probability-weighted cash-flow IRRs about **23%/18%**. These substantial aggregate returns coexist with a negative arithmetic mean of scenario annualized returns (about −16%) because the downside wipes out the position. No dividend is assumed. Retained operating cash is added once and debt is deducted once. Share-settling the acquisition at today's price reduces the base IRR with a 20% exit discount to about **24%**. These results describe conditional payoffs; they do not remove refinancing or legal risk.

Observable milestones: H2 2026 must show the cost reduction in cash operating profit without further deterioration of data revenue; the 2026 annual filing must clarify controls and the legal case. By early 2027, identify the actual cash/share acquisition-settlement election and sufficient covenant headroom. Obtain a credible refinancing path well before February 2028. Data revenue below $45m annualized with poor retention, persistent marketing declines exceeding 15%, an injunction limiting the core data product, or incremental cash claims above available liquidity invalidates the recovery case. Each has an explicit adverse valuation or funding consequence above. Until the rights/controls/financing links are supported, the large modeled gap is a research lead, not a confirmed recommendation.

**Shared dependencies and remaining gaps.** Opera and Grandstand both depend on online user acquisition and major platform policies; their risks are correlated even though one is a browser and one sells sports data and marketing. Independent data-supply counts, negotiated prices and customer-level retention are not public. OPay's carrying valuation cannot be fully reconstructed from independent investee audited cash flows. GRSD's legal outcome, current covenant EBITDA adjustments and debt renewal are not settled by a press release. These unresolved items are not filled with synthetic consensus or precise capacity figures. Criteo and Pediatrix are the strongest additional watchlist names here; both require deeper normalized owner-cash work before being classified as extreme opportunities.
