The Stellantis causal chain is: replacement demand and a more affordable product range → customer purchases financed from household/fleet budgets → higher plant utilization after capacity reductions → modest shipment and contribution-margin recovery → cash after research, factories, warranty settlements and financing → residual value for today's ordinary shareholders. Customer purchases and initial shipment recovery are observed; the duration of share stabilization, achievable savings and later cash conversion are forecasts. There is no industry-wide capacity shortage in the base case. The constraint is competitive product execution and cost absorption, with labor agreements and platform changes slowing the supply adjustment. Competitors can absorb cost savings through lower selling prices. H1 European shipment growth alongside essentially unchanged operating losses is direct evidence that volumes alone do not create value. Industrial FCFF equals EBIT×(1−tax)+D&A−capex−change in working capital. Base tax is 27%, USD nominal WACC 11%, terminal growth 1.5%, and incremental terminal ROIC 10%; 2036–2040 explicitly converges to sustainable reinvestment with a cash replacement floor. Captive discount rate is 12.5%; its required equity and distributions are separately modeled annually. The downside and upside use 13%/10% industrial WACC and 0.5%/2% terminal growth. Probabilities are 30%/50%/20%, judgments reflecting operational recovery evidence, repeated failed promises and a genuine possibility that ordinary equity has little residual value. They are not market-implied probabilities. Moving ten percentage points from upside to downside cuts weighted value substantially; valuations.json provides all scenario values, return cashflows, terminal contributions and WACC/g sensitivities. The probability-weighted intrinsic gap must not be confused with a guaranteed return or with an arithmetic average of scenario IRRs.

“The current price can be justified if” base shipments and spending occur, but industrial margins run about 0.36 percentage points below my base path, including approximately 4.26% in 2030. This parallel-shift reverse DCF changes later sustainable reinvestment when required; it does not reveal unique market expectations. “My forecast differs because” some utilization recovery and ordinary regional margins appear achievable. “The evidence supporting that difference is” observed shipment recovery, explicit footprint actions and contemporaneous competitor margins, counterbalanced by Europe's failure to monetize volume so far. “The resulting difference in value is” approximately $1.84 per share in the base case, or 34%. Direct consensus for matching segments, accounting perimeter and investment spending was unavailable. That disagreement is too small relative to execution risk to establish an extreme bargain.

The strongest opposing case is that product and quality problems are structural, competitors match lower prices, plant reductions preserve labor costs, consumers resist expensive vehicles, and the finance book requires continued parent capital. A one-percentage-point margin reduction takes base value to $2.21; another €1 billion annual investment takes it to $3.39. Combining 75bp lower margins, another €1 billion annual investment, 12.5% WACC, 0.5% growth and a 25% captive-value haircut eliminates common value. Removing captive value alone gives $5.23. The 2024–2025 history is particularly adverse evidence: reported industrial cash outflow was €6.045 billion and €4.525 billion despite an earlier expectation of positive 2025 cash generation. Semiconductor-era vehicle scarcity and rich mix did not protect subsequent returns once availability normalized and inventory correction began. Current cost/product repair differs from that shortage episode, but it must earn its cash recovery rather than inherit old margins. Observable tests are FY2026 cash research plus capital spending remaining within the disclosed framework; 2027 industrial cash outflow narrowing, with positive cash conversion by2029 after actual supplier settlements (later than management expects); Europe producing at least a1.5% adjusted operating margin by2028 without rising incentives; North America exceeding 4% by 2028; and clear factory reduction milestones before 2030. Persistent negative industrial FCF in2030, another multi-billion-euro reset without funding, renewed equity issuance, or recurring investment above the stress path invalidates the base case. Retained cash is counted once, separately from dividends and the captive residual value.
