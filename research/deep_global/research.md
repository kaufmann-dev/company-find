This extension identifies a substantial, evidence-supported present-value gap in PDD, and a smaller gap in Vipshop. It does not establish exceptional five-year returns without a change in the market discount. The economic starting point is limited household spending and migration toward value and online shopping. January–July 2026 Chinese retail sales rose only 1.2%; online goods sales rose 4.6%, online clothing 5.8%, and brand-specialty-store sales declined. These figures support some channel substitution, not a consumption boom. The online goods-and-services series has a broader scope and must not be added to online goods as incremental spending. The payers are households buying goods and merchants spending part of their gross profit on customer acquisition and distribution. Better ad matching can reduce merchant cost per sale, but competition can return the savings to merchants rather than expand platform fees. See [NBS, August 18 release, January–July retail section](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260819_1965078.html).

There is no verified physical shortage central to either finalist. Merchandise, digital advertising and fulfillment have responsive competing supply. Suppliers can use several channels; customers can switch with little cost. PDD's base case consequently combines rising activity with falling fee/mix per activity, and Vipshop's case allows existing infrastructure to handle a lower order count. A claim that either owns a persistent supply bottleneck would be unsupported. The broader twelve-name screen follows household consumption into advertising, credit, logistics and telecom connectivity, and compares tobacco's mature cash returns. Each name has a dated primary role, mechanism and investigation decision in screened.json; only PDD and Vipshop have full new operating DCFs. Rejection of research priority for the others is not a conclusion that their prices exceed intrinsic value.
