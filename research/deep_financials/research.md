**Financial-sector comparisons — 8 September 2026**

Twelve current-price comparisons were investigated before deciding where another detailed model would be useful. The operational demand is retirement protection, employee benefits, mortgage credit, catastrophe risk transfer and Latin American trade finance. Premiums and deposits fund insurance or bank assets; they are not owner revenue available for distribution. Customer liabilities, hedges, reserve deterioration and regulatory capital stand between accounting earnings and shareholder cash.

The clearest accounting trap is Jackson: its 3 August release reports second-quarter adjusted operating earnings of $513 million, but parent free cash flow of $287 million. The latter includes subsidiary remittances after operating-company capital needs; annualizing the former as distributable cash would exaggerate the valuation. Its approximately 0.89 times adjusted book price is interesting, but insufficient to establish an exceptional discount. [Jackson second-quarter results, 3 August 2026, capitalization and cash-flow reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1822993/000182299326000123/exhibit991-2q26.htm).

Genworth offers an asset-discount lead through its Enact stake, but Enact's traded price is not an independent intrinsic valuation. Parent expenses, CareScout investment and the closed insurance block consume economics. The $103 million second-quarter Enact remittance compares with only $215 million of parent liquidity. Legal isolation and possible regulatory constraints need a complete holding-company model before treating the stake discount as cash accessible to GNW investors. [Genworth second-quarter results, 5 August 2026](https://investor.genworth.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1060/genworth-financial-announces-second-quarter-2026-results).

Reinsurance supply is capital as well as qualified underwriting capacity. Strong profits attract additional risk capital, and a quiet catastrophe period is not normal loss experience. Everest's 7.1% decline in core written premiums and RenaissanceRe's reserve releases constrain the inference from their attractive current ROEs. Mortgage insurance similarly needs sufficient capital for a downturn; Enact's current price is above book against approximately 13% adjusted ROE. These can be good businesses without meeting an extreme-discount hurdle. The individual dated sources and numerical cross-checks are recorded in screened.json.

Corporate actions change the investment. Brighthouse's $70 cash deal versus the sampled $53.17 creates a 31.7% gross spread, but the agreement extends from 6 September to 6 December if insurance approvals remain outstanding. This is an unresolved completion-probability and break-value problem. Corebridge's agreed merger gives its holders one new-parent share while Equitable holders receive 1.55516; a standalone Corebridge DCF would omit the actual future business combination. Neither is promoted from a headline spread or low earnings multiple to a confirmed investment. [Brighthouse 5 August release, merger section](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1685040/000168504026000026/q22026bhfearningspressrele.htm), [Corebridge/Equitable 26 March agreement, transaction terms](https://s201.q4cdn.com/405089319/files/doc_news/2026/03/26/Corebridge-Financial-and-Equitable-Holdings-Announce-Transformational-Merger.pdf).
