Monetary inputs are USD millions. The common report contains the synchronized valuation results and price timestamps. Each future input in build_inputs.py is an analyst assumption; the latest company outlook is labeled management guidance. No current broker consensus dataset was obtained.

Digital commerce creates paid work in authorization, fraud prevention, checkout, settlement, account servicing and reconciliation. Merchants fund it from transaction expenses because successful, reliable payments support sales and reduce administrative costs. Banks pay processing providers from operating and technology budgets. These are recurring purchases tied to activity, with migration work and replacement software investment also required. The capitalized value of the providers depends on the fee retained after paying networks, distribution partners, engineers and credit losses.

The demand evidence is stronger than the evidence for provider pricing power. US retail e-commerce rose 12.2% year over year in the second quarter, but that is nominal retail spending, not global payment volume or an extra revenue pool to add to company sales. Visa's quarterly transactions grew 10%, while Adyen's first-half processed volume grew 24% and revenue 19%. These corroborate activity and competition, not a consensus forecast. The consumer payment diary reports relatively stable instrument preferences; its 2026 publication describes 2025 observations. Efficiency may improve authorization and processing costs, but competing merchants can capture the saving. I assume neither automatic demand destruction nor a sufficient rebound to preserve provider margins. [Census, 18 August 2026](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html), [Visa, 28 July 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000103/q32026earningsrelease.htm), [Adyen, 13 August 2026](https://www.adyen.com/press-and-media/adyen-publishes-h1-2026-financial-results-3wjne), [Federal Reserve Financial Services, 2 June 2026](https://www.frbservices.org/news/fed360/issues/060226/cash-2026-diary-of-consumer-payment-choice).

PayPal's causal chain is digital transaction activity [observed] → merchant willingness to offer its checkout and processing [observed, retention uncertain] → payment volumes [observed history, forecast assumptions] → fee capture [observed declining relative to volume] → margins and investment [forecast] → free cash and present equity value [calculated]. The network is usable; there is no quantified physical supply shortage to capitalize. The constraint is maintaining customer preference and monetization in competition with wallets, integrated processors and merchant-owned checkout experiences.

The annual PayPal model is active accounts × annual transactions per account × average transaction value × transaction revenue yield, plus other value-added services. Accounts are a stock; transactions are an annual flow. June's 439 million accounts, 13.225 billion first-half transactions and $950.403 billion first-half TPV anchor the model. The 2026 forecast assumes 440 million accounts, 62 transactions per account and a $72.50 ticket, producing $1.978 trillion TPV. The future ticket includes nominal inflation. The base path moderates intensity growth and allows persistent fee erosion of 1.7% a year. Because Braintree and Venmo activity cannot be treated as equally profitable to branded checkout, the model applies no separate high-margin brand premium. [PayPal July 28 10-Q, note 2 and MD&A pp51–52](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000082/pypl-20260630.htm).

Base operating margin falls to 15.5% in 2027 and recovers to 16.5%, below 2025's 18.3%. This requires modest execution success, not a return to peak digital-commerce growth. The downside combines account attrition, weak usage growth, 3.5% annual fee erosion, lower lending revenues and a 10% mature margin. The upside needs better engagement and a 19% mature margin. Probabilities are 30/50/20: current fee/mix deterioration earns substantial adverse weight, while the long operating record supports a more likely stable franchise than collapse. They are subjective judgments, not statistical frequencies. All scenarios deduct capital expenditure, including capitalized compensation, and funding for growth in the loan book.

The balance bridge uses corporate cash and short-term investments, then reserves $3 billion operating liquidity and the $2 billion gap between customer obligations and matching receivables/accounts. It gives no value to $4.009 billion of long-term investments; valuing all at book would add approximately $4.66 per diluted share. That deliberately conservative exclusion is shown as a sensitivity, not a claim that these assets are worthless. The September dividend's record date predates this valuation date, so new buyers receive no credit for it. Current diluted shares use the July cover count plus observed incremental awards. Forecast EBIT retains stock compensation; adding it back would overstate owner cash. The 2023–25 cash-flow record is variable, including credit receivable timing, so one strong quarter is not annualized. [PayPal 2025 10-K, filed 3 February 2026, financial statements and compensation note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000024/pypl-20251231.htm), [July 28 earnings release, balance sheet and dividend program](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm).

The price-implied example holds base volumes, fees, reinvestment, discount rate and terminal growth fixed, then solves a parallel margin change. A roughly 15% consolidated margin in 2030 can justify the initial snapshot price, compared with my 16.5% forecast. The forecast difference is real but small relative to the downside and uncertainty. A 100-basis-point higher base WACC, or moving ten probability points from upside to downside, largely removes the apparent headroom. The strongest opposing case to rejection is that profitable branded engagement stabilizes, Venmo monetizes and buybacks executed below intrinsic value add per-share value; the model credits no speculative buyback accretion. The measurable test is transaction-margin-dollar growth excluding balance interest, not TPV alone. Two successive quarters of declining transaction-margin dollars despite growing TPV, sustained active-account attrition above 2%, or GAAP operating margin below 13% invalidate the stable-franchise base path. The complete downside reprices all these variables together.

Fiserv's causal chain is bank/merchant payment activity [observed] → processing and platform contracts [observed] → volumes and account retention [partly observed, retention unresolved] → distribution/service costs and fee yield [observed pressure, forecast uncertain] → corporate cash after reinvestment and debt interest [calculated]. A payment network's customer can also be an intermediary supplier or competitor. There is no additive market opportunity from counting a transaction once at Visa, again at PayPal and again at Fiserv: the models allocate separate retained fees out of the same underlying spending.

Fiserv disclosed $4.6 trillion 2025 merchant GPV, 3.9 million small-business relationships and approximately one million enterprise locations. The investor-day transcript reconciles 2.7 million directly served SMBs with another 1.2 million processing-only relationships. This distinction prevents double counting customers. Reported regular throughput and a test above three times that throughput do not establish annual usable capacity. The merchant demand sensitivity begins with the GPV anchor and projects independent growth of 2%, 5.5% or 8% after 2026. Its all-in revenue yield includes software/services; it is not a posted acquiring fee. Absolute account counts and price/volume attribution for Financial Solutions were not verified, so that segment remains an explicitly labeled revenue sensitivity. [Fiserv investor day, filed 15 May 2026, pp5–9](https://investors.fiserv.com/static-files/af17ec6f-4ca7-4261-804c-2fb24b9d2430).

In the base model, Merchant and Financial margins settle at 32% and 40% before corporate costs. Acquisition-amortization runoff reduces both corporate expense and the D&A addback. Maintenance and growth software investment are both deducted. The June filing also discloses $595 million of first-half financed asset additions outside cash capex (note16: $126 million software/intangibles, $19 million hardware and $450 million finance leases). I include an assumed $1.190 billion full-year amount, falling to $650 million from2030, without crediting extra lease borrowing. This materially lowers owner cash; the model also reserves continuing contract, lending and cash-tax working-capital requirements. The annual forecast includes zero-margin postage reimbursements, which must not earn the platform margin. This is less profitable than management's recovery ambition but more favorable than uninterrupted deterioration. Q2's 19.2% consolidated GAAP margin, against 30.7% a year earlier, is concrete evidence that extra transactions do not automatically create owner cash. [Fiserv August 6 results, segment and GAAP reconciliations](https://investors.fiserv.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fiserv-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

The downside combines falling Financial revenue, merchant pricing pressure, expensive service remediation and persistent capital needs. At $28.058 billion principal debt, including financing obligations and restored unamortized discounts/hedge adjustments, common residual value can be exhausted without revenue disappearing. This is a valuation stress outcome, not a prediction of an imminent bankruptcy. Settlement cash and restricted cash are excluded from excess cash. Noncontrolling claims are reserved above book; nonoperating affiliate investments receive no speculative premium. Shares include both observed current dilution and a conservative allowance for presently antidilutive awards. These adjustments are individually too small to rescue the base valuation. [Fiserv June 10-Q, filed August 6, notes1,8,11–12 and18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/798354/000079835426000031/fisv-20260630.htm).

Neither payments comparison is a confirmed recommendation. The most favorable modeled case is insufficient without plausible probabilities, and a cheap earnings multiple is not enough. Shared dependencies are household spending, financial-system reliability, regulation, merchant fee bargaining and efficient software execution. PayPal has more funding resilience; Fiserv has substantially greater creditor exposure. Remaining evidence gaps are detailed product profitability, current retention cohorts, true maintenance/growth investment splits, at-date treasury shares and post-interim cash movements. All are exposed in the inputs rather than filled with invented facts.
