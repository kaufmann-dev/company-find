USD is the valuation currency. All forward operating numbers are analyst scenarios, not consensus. The final synchronized Massive snapshot is $26.115, minute timestamp15:02UTC, captured15:18:28UTC. The completed comparison finds a plausible value gap at Comcast but insufficient confidence to recommend it. Final downside/base/upside values are approximately $0/$33.14/$73.32, weighted $31.24 at30%/50%/20%, or19.6% above price and16.4% margin of safety (1 − price/fair value). A ten-point probability transfer from upside to downside reduces that weighted value to $23.90. A less extreme combined operating stress produces $15.12. These are calculated outcomes under explicit assumptions, not likelihoods inferred from stock-price behavior. The central valuation engine extends normalized cash flows through2040 before its terminal value; final machine-readable results accompany this note.

## Start with the activity and the customer budget

Households need reliable connectivity for work, communication, entertainment and administration. Usage can rise faster than households, but a flat-rate broadband subscription does not automatically monetize extra traffic. Better compression and more efficient network equipment reduce the capacity required per hour of use; more viewing can consume part of that saving. Neither effect assures higher billed revenue. The relevant financial driver is paying connections multiplied by realized monthly revenue, with network investment required to preserve reliability and competitive speed.

Customers compare a combined fixed-and-mobile bill. Comcast can retain broadband customers by bundling wireless and converting introductory free lines into paid relationships. This moves some economics from separate broadband ARPU into wireless revenue. Whether total contribution improves depends on retention, mobile network wholesale costs, device subsidies and acquisition expense. The latest reported facts give an early test: Q2 domestic broadband revenue declined 5.5%, residential EBITDA declined 8%, domestic wireless service revenue increased 14.2%, and the combined domestic broadband-plus-wireless service revenue still declined 3.2%. The declining combined metric is more informative than wireless additions alone. [Comcast July 23, 2026 trending schedule, pp.1–4](https://www.cmcsa.com/static-files/b269b35e-94ff-43d2-8f05-858c92bb7488).

Supply can respond. AT&T already reached 38.6 million fiber locations in June, and management's funded capital program targets more than 60 million by 2030. Its stated annual capital investment is $23–24 billion during 2026–2028. These are national locations, not additional competing Comcast households: overlaps, already served locations, customer take-up and joint-venture ownership must not be added indiscriminately. [AT&T July 22, 2026 release, Second-Quarter Highlights and Outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/732717/000073271726000294/t-2q2026exhibit991.htm).

The relevant mechanism is the replacement advantage of an existing network and a bundle that customers find economical. It is not a persistent shortage. Comcast's hybrid fiber/coax network can be upgraded; equipment, field labor, customer devices and new passings require capital. Its domestic wireless offering uses Verizon's network and its own Wi-Fi; business wireless also uses T-Mobile starting in 2026. Thus mobile network suppliers capture some of the revenue. Contract unit costs and local competitor overlap were not disclosed in the material retrieved. [Comcast February 3, 2026 10-K, Business pp.2–5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166691/000162828026004994/cmcsa-20251231.htm).

## The independent annual operating model

`operating_drivers.json` contains annual customers, monthly realized revenue, implied penetration, Peacock subscribers and revenue, and the constituent residential revenue calculations for every scenario. `build_inputs.py` contains all equations and independently chosen arrays. Subscriber numbers are annual average installed customer stocks, not annual additions. Network passings are an addressable stock, not usable throughput capacity. Measured local spare radio capacity, overlapping fiber locations, and provider-level usable throughput are unavailable, so the file expressly does not assert a verified shortage or its duration.

Base broadband average subscribers decline from 28.5 million in 2026 to 26.6 million in 2030 and 26.2 million in 2035. Monthly realized revenue moves from $74 to $72.50 in 2027–2028, then recovers to $74.30 in 2030 and $82 in 2035. This assumes the introductory-price reset eventually ends; it does not assume a return to the old subscriber peak. Wireless average lines grow from 10.15 million to 16.4 million in 2030 and 20.5 million in 2035, with service ARPU $33/$34.50/$36. Equipment revenue is modeled separately, at $161.60 per average line per year; this is a sales-mix assumption, not equipment margin. Video revenue declines 6% annually, other legacy residential revenue 7%, and international connectivity grows 4%. These assumptions produce approximately flat aggregate nominal residential economics as mobile and international growth absorb video decline.

Affordability is tested at the bundle level: base annual broadband revenue per connection is $888 initially and $984 in 2035, versus $396 and $432 annual wireless revenue per line. The household can own several mobile lines, so lines are not households. The model does not claim all incremental wireless spending is a new consumer budget; much migrates from other carriers. Devices financed over several years create receivables, included in working-capital investment. Comcast's recent free-line conversion evidence is management's interpretation, with no disclosed mature cohort margin or churn curve. It remains the decisive unresolved link.

Business connectivity revenue grows from $10.7 billion in 2026 to $12.97 billion in 2030 and $16.24 billion in 2035, with EBITDA margin 56–57%. This is a moderate extrapolation from current revenue and margin evidence; there is no unsupported unit/customer-count decomposition where none is disclosed. Peacock average paid subscribers reach 59 million in 2030 and 70 million in 2035. Total monthly revenue per subscriber includes both subscriptions and advertising, so ads are not added a second time. The base assumes Peacock approximately breaks even in 2027 and reaches an 18% EBITDA margin in 2035.

The media forecast separately reduces recurring linear revenue 4% annually and includes a $2 billion even-year major-event revenue allowance, with corresponding costs implicit in margins. That allowance is a simplifying assumption: Olympics are not identical each cycle and FIFA spending is not an annual annuity. Rights owners have bargaining power. NBCUniversal's NBA agreement starts with the 2025–2026 season and lasts eleven years; future renewal costs cannot be assumed away. [NBCUniversal July 24, 2024 announcement](https://www.nbcuniversal.com/article/nba-and-wnba-return-nbcuniversal-11-year-agreement-regular-season-and-playoff-basketball-nbc-peacock).

Studios retain cyclical title economics rather than annualizing a successful quarter. Base theme-park revenue rises from $10.3 billion to $12.9 billion in 2030 and $16.9 billion in 2035, with the UK park opening assumption around the disclosed 2031 target and lower opening-year margins. Absolute attendance and usable park-capacity data were not disclosed; these are revenue scenarios, not a fabricated gate-count model. Capital expenditures remain high through construction and then explicitly transition toward replacement and growth reinvestment.

The supplied annual arrays give each operating segment gross revenue and an allocated GAAP-like EBIT margin. The reported segments include intercompany distribution and licensing revenue; a separate negative revenue reconciliation removes that revenue exactly once. Net headquarters/corporate/elimination expense is modeled outside segment margins. D&A allocation across segments is an analyst allocation because segment GAAP EBIT is not disclosed; the total reconciles. The attractive Peacock and parks assumptions are never assigned to the entire company.

## Financial history, capital and current shareholder claims

Three years of as-reported history are stored in `inputs.json`, alongside 2024–2025 pro forma operating comparisons. These two series are deliberately distinguished. As-reported 2025 includes Versant and Germany; the July 2026 recast schedule removes them. Pro forma Media EBITDA was negative $123 million in 2024 and positive $164 million in 2025, while pro forma residential EBITDA fell from $27.06 billion to $26.364 billion. Those adverse conditions materially constrain the modeled recovery. [July 23, 2026 trending schedule, pp.1–3](https://www.cmcsa.com/static-files/b269b35e-94ff-43d2-8f05-858c92bb7488).

The bridge uses $7.661 billion cash, $90.381 billion debt, and a $2.5 billion analyst operating-cash reserve. Deferred compensation is a separate senior economic claim: the latest detailed obligation was $5.097 billion at December 2025, with $662 million life-insurance cash surrender value credited separately. June investments of $7.828 billion receive a 50% value credit, and the $1.1 billion unfunded Atairos commitment is fully reserved. That conservative commitment reserve does not credit any assets purchased with future contributions. The model also deducts $185 million redeemable minority interest and a $1.5 billion analyst reserve for nonredeemable minority fair value. [June 2026 10-Q, balance sheet and Note 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166691/000162828026049360/cmcsa-20260630.htm); [2025 10-K, Notes 8 and 11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166691/000162828026004994/cmcsa-20251231.htm).

Universal Beijing is fully consolidated despite only 30% ownership. Its $3.7 billion June debt is already in group debt and is not deducted twice. Book minority interest is not assumed to equal economic value. An extra $4 billion noncommon-claim reserve reduces base value by about $1.13 per share. Eliminating the investment-portfolio value credit reduces it by about $1.10. These uncertainties are meaningful but smaller than competitive operating risk.

The July 15 current share count includes 3,539.192198 million Class A plus 9.444375 million Class B shares. Add the latest quarter's six-million dilutive effect as a proxy for existing awards. Another 291 million potential shares were antidilutive at the quarter's price. Modeling all of those as free additional shares, without exercise proceeds, is an intentionally excessive dilution stress; it reduces base value from $33.14 to $30.64. Expensed SBC remains in margins; there is no SBC addback and no future buyback accretion. Corporate cost assumptions also include $200 million annually for capitalized SBC: H1 cash-flow SBC exceeded income-statement SBC by $90 million, so cash-capex accounting must not silently omit that economic cost. [June 2026 10-Q, cover, cash flow and Note 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166691/000162828026049360/cmcsa-20260630.htm).

Versant was distributed January 2 and Germany sold May 31; a purchaser today does not receive the already-separated Versant shares. The announced NBCUniversal/Sky separation is different: the current purchaser owns both prospective economic claims. Forecast their combined cash generation, charge separation costs and ignore any hoped-for multiple premium. Precise debt allocation, financing terms, stranded costs and separation conditions remain unresolved. Buybacks were paused June 29. The pending ITV acquisition is reserved at its full £1.6 billion maximum consideration in 2027, translated at Massive's September 7 GBPUSD daily close of 1.3546 into $2.16736 billion. No acquired cash earnings are credited in the main model; an extra $150 million annual EBIT from 2028, without additional investment, adds only about $0.34 per share in a sensitivity. This is a conservative reserve, not a claim that ITV is worthless. [June 2026 10-Q, Note 6 and MD&A Liquidity/Capital Resources](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166691/000162828026049360/cmcsa-20260630.htm).

Capex includes property, capitalized software and other cash intangible investment. Program costs remain in operating expenses; changes in program inventory/production timing enter working capital. Maintenance/growth splitting is not disclosed sufficiently to label exact amounts as facts. The model deducts all cash investment. Base net working-capital outflow alternates $650/$350 million with the event cycle and includes wireless financing needs. Beginning in 2032 capital expenditure moves gradually to a 2035 net reinvestment level of NOPAT × terminal growth / terminal ROIC. This prevents an indefinitely favorable gap between depreciation and replacement spending. Leases treated as operating expense are not also deducted as debt.

Tax rates rise from 20% of operating profit in 2026 to 25.5% by 2030. The early cash-tax benefit is therefore not capitalized forever. H1 2026 cash tax was only $559 million; annualizing that payment would exaggerate sustainable FCF. Debt is held fixed with an assumed 4.75% financing rate, reflecting eventual refinancing rather than asserting today's cash coupon is 4.75%. Operating cash generation covers modeled interest/dividends in all scenarios through the five-year horizon, but a long-run equity impairment can occur without an immediate funding crisis. Deferred-compensation settlement is excluded from future NWC because its present claim has already been deducted.

## Valuation, reverse case and attempted disproof

The shared `model.py` implements annual FCFF, an explicit transition and discounted terminal economics, then deducts all senior claims. Base nominal USD WACC is 8.75%, versus 10% in the combined adverse case and 8% in the favorable case. These are analyst required rates, not measured consensus costs of capital. Mature connectivity plus cyclical media, substantial debt and execution risk justify a discount rate well above the debt cost. Terminal growth is 0.5%/2%/2.5% and incremental ROIC 10%/13%/16%. There is no terminal scarcity margin.

Current basic market equity is $92.67 billion; enterprise value after the operating-cash reserve and stated noncommon/assets adjustments is $181.20 billion (conventional full-cash bridge $178.70 billion). Base terminal value at2040 contributes 39.6% of operating EV; the preceding explicit forecasts and transition contribute the rest. Present fair value is distinct from the modeled September2031 residual combined equity value and dividends.

The current price can be justified if consolidated EBIT margins are approximately 1.98 percentage points below the base path in every modeled year, with the base revenue/capital schedule and 8.75% WACC unchanged. That combination has a 2030 margin near16.69%, versus the analyst base 18.67%. It is one valid reverse DCF, not a uniquely identified market forecast.

My forecast differs because the base assumes eventual paid-wireless conversion, slower broadband losses, modest business-service growth and improved Peacock economics despite declining legacy video. The evidence supporting that difference is the growing wireless base, actual Q2 Peacock profitability and business connectivity margins. The evidence opposing it is more direct for the near term: aggregate convergence revenue and residential earnings still decline. The resulting base difference in present value is approximately $7.03 per share; probability weighting reduces the difference to $5.12. Directly available full-year consensus forecasts were unavailable, so none are invented.

Shareholder returns use dated dividends and future combined equity claims after five years. Undistributed after-interest cash accumulates once; buybacks are neither additional distributions nor assumed share accretion. Base dividends stay at $1.32 annually; adverse dividends fall to $0.90 in 2028. Year-end payment aggregation and the initial remaining-year stub are disclosed approximations. The scenario annualized returns are approximately −45.5%/+17.3%/+35.1%. The arithmetic probability-weighted IRR was about2.0%, while the IRR on probability-weighted dated cash flows was about15.4%; these are different statistics and the favorable second number does not remove the severe lower-tail risk.

The strongest opposing case combines continued fiber/FWA share loss, a permanent reduction in broadband realized revenue, limited free-line conversion, rising mobile wholesale costs, expensive sports rights, disappointing parks utilization and higher separation cost. Revenue need not shrink quickly for equity to suffer: margins and reinvestment can deteriorate while national internet use grows. In the adverse annual model broadband subscribers fall to 21.2 million and ARPU only $72 by 2035, residential margin settles at 33%, Peacock gains little contribution, and the cost of capital rises. Senior claims absorb the modeled enterprise value and common value rounds to zero. This is a structural impairment stress, not a forecast of imminent bankruptcy.

More moderate falsification is reproducible in `additional_checks.py`: holding broadband ARPU at $70 from 2027 with 75% incremental contribution on foregone revenue gives $27.38 base value; recurrent Peacock losses of $500 million give $27.40; all investment 15% higher gives $23.58. Combine $70 broadband ARPU, another $1 billion permanent annual separation cost, and 15% higher capital spending and value falls to $15.12. A 1-point higher base WACC alone gives approximately $25.67 at 2% terminal growth. These tests remove the safety margin without requiring a complete collapse.

The historical comparison is the company's own 2024–2026 deterioration after network demand had already grown: existing infrastructure and rising data use did not prevent competitive monetization pressure. A purported long-lived connectivity bottleneck is specifically rejected. The key milestones are paid conversion and combined revenue stabilization over the next four to six quarters; annual average broadband subscribers above roughly 27.4 million in 2028; ARPU at least around $72.50; Peacock sustaining profitability outside a major-event quarter by 2027–2028; and disclosed separation financing/costs by the planned mid-2027 completion. Persistent combined revenue contraction above 3%, residential EBITDA margin below 35% through 2028, or capital investment staying more than 15% above the modeled path invalidates the base recovery and calls for revaluation.

## Screen and evidence gaps

`screened.json` records Comcast, Charter, AT&T and T-Mobile, with dated primary role evidence, mechanisms, current Massive quotes and reasons to advance or stop. Charter's leveraged equity may be cheap but its Cox transaction requires an independent claim/capital bridge. AT&T is a credible further-research alternative at an undemanding simple earnings screen, but acquired fiber/JV and spectrum financing plus copper retirement need a full model. T-Mobile's faster service growth does not establish undervaluation; its headline Core EBITDA excludes SBC. No price-based intrinsic conclusion is claimed for these three without a DCF.

`sources.json` registers the decisive primary disclosures. Remaining material gaps are mature free-line conversion profitability/churn, local competing supply overlap and spare wireless capacity, exact MVNO economics, sustainable post-NBA content economics, absolute park attendance/capacity, final separation financing and awards conversion. Governance also matters: Class B has one-third of voting power with a very small economic ownership share. The claim bridge has conservative reserves, but those reserves cannot resolve the central operating assumptions. Comcast therefore remains an unconfirmed research candidate, not a selected investment.
